How to Register as a Florida Vendor in MyFloridaMarketPlace

MyFloridaMarketPlace (MFMP) is how Florida state agencies buy. If you want to sell to the State of Florida, your vendor registration in MFMP is step one — here is how it works, in plain order.

What MyFloridaMarketPlace is

MyFloridaMarketPlace is the State of Florida's online procurement system, run by the Department of Management Services. State agencies use it to post solicitations, find vendors, and issue purchase orders. Registering as a vendor creates the official record agencies use to identify your business, match you to what they are buying, and notify you about opportunities. If your ambitions are federal instead, that is a different door — SAM.gov, covered in our SAM.gov registration guide. Many Florida businesses register in both, because state and federal buyers do not share a system.

Before you register: gather your information

  • Legal business name and business structure — as they appear on your Florida and IRS records. If your company is organized in Florida, its Division of Corporations (Sunbiz) record should be current and consistent.
  • Tax ID (EIN/FEIN) and the W-9 information the state will use for payments and tax reporting.
  • Business and remittance addresses, plus the email address where solicitation notices should go — pick one that a person actually reads every business day.
  • A list of what you sell, in plain words. You will translate this into commodity codes during registration, so think it through first.
  • Any certifications you hold — Florida certifies businesses owned by minorities, women, and veterans through its Office of Supplier Development; if you qualify, have the details ready.

The registration steps

1. Go to the MFMP vendor portal.
Start at vendor.myfloridamarketplace.com and choose the option to register as a new vendor. This is the official state site — make sure the address matches exactly.

2. Enter your business and tax information.
Provide the legal name, tax ID, addresses, and contact details you gathered. Type them to match your records exactly; the state verifies vendor information against its files.

3. Choose your commodity codes carefully.
Commodity codes describe your goods and services in the system's vocabulary. Select every code that honestly fits what you sell — this step decides which solicitations you are notified about and which searches find you.

4. Add certifications and preferences that apply.
If your business holds a Florida certification (for example through the Office of Supplier Development) or qualifies for programs the registration asks about, record them here so agencies searching by those criteria can find you.

5. Complete account setup and confirm.
Finish the remaining account details, submit, and save your confirmation and login information. Registration terms are shown on the portal itself — there is no separate sign-up fee just to register, and any terms that apply to actual sales under state contracts are stated there, so read them as you go.

6. Verify you can be found.
After your registration is processed, search the vendor directory the way a buyer would — by your commodity codes and location — and confirm your business appears with correct contact details.

How Florida state solicitations reach you after registration

Registered vendors can watch for solicitations inside MFMP and receive notices tied to their commodity codes; agencies also advertise formal solicitations publicly. In practice, the vendors who win state work treat it like our guide on finding your first government contract describes at the federal level: check postings on a rhythm, read each solicitation completely, mind the questions deadline (which falls before the response deadline), and submit exactly what is asked, in the format asked, before the deadline. When you respond, having your one-page capability statement ready makes introductions and follow-ups with agency staff far easier.

Two habits matter as much as the registration itself. First, keep your record current — an old email address or lapsed certification quietly removes you from notices and searches. Update MFMP whenever your address, contact, or codes change, and keep your Sunbiz record in good standing. Second, remember MFMP is state agencies only. Florida's counties, cities, school districts, and universities buy through their own portals and vendor lists. If you want local work — and for many small businesses, local work is the most reachable first government revenue — register with the counties and cities you can actually serve, one by one. There is no single registration that covers every buyer, whatever any website implies.

Questions

Who needs to register in MyFloridaMarketPlace?

Any business that wants to sell goods or services to Florida state agencies. Registration creates your vendor record, lets agencies find you, and is how you receive and respond to state solicitations.

What are commodity codes, and why do they matter?

Commodity codes describe what your business sells. Agencies search and notify vendors by these codes, so choosing the codes that honestly match your goods and services is how relevant solicitations reach you — and how buyers find you for quotes.

Does registering in MFMP guarantee I will get state work?

No. Registration makes you eligible to be found and to respond; you still compete for each solicitation on your price, your qualifications, and how completely you follow the instructions.

Does MFMP registration cover Florida counties and cities too?

No. MyFloridaMarketPlace is the state agency system. Counties, cities, school districts, and other local bodies run their own vendor registrations and procurement portals, each separate from MFMP.
