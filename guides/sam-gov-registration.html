How to Register on SAM.gov: Step by Step (2026)

SAM.gov registration is the front door to federal contracts and grants. It is free, it is done entirely at sam.gov, and most delays come from a handful of avoidable mistakes. Here is the process in order.

Why you need a SAM.gov registration

SAM.gov — the System for Award Management — is the federal government's official registration system for organizations that want to do business with it. If you want to bid on federal contracts, receive a federal grant, or get paid by a federal agency, your business generally must be registered and active in SAM.gov first.

One fact matters before anything else: registration itself is free. You never have to pay the government — or anyone else — for the registration. Companies do sometimes pay for help with the setup, the way people pay an accountant to prepare a return they could file themselves. That help is optional — this guide is written so you can do it all yourself.

Before you start: gather these first

Registration goes smoothly when your paperwork matches itself. Before you open the site, have these items in front of you:

  • Your legal business name — exactly as it appears on your IRS documents and your state incorporation or organization records. Not your trade name, not your DBA.
  • Your EIN (or TIN) — the tax ID exactly as the IRS has it on file.
  • Your physical business address — the address tied to your business records.
  • Bank account information — federal payments are made electronically, so you will enter the bank routing and account numbers that should receive payments.
  • Your NAICS codes — the industry codes that describe what your company sells. You can pick several that honestly fit your work.
  • A business email address you control — validation notices and your annual renewal reminders go there.

If your legal name on your state records does not match the name the IRS has for your EIN, fix that first or expect the registration to stall in validation. This single mismatch is the most common reason first-time registrations sit for weeks.

The registration steps, in order

1. Create your Login.gov account.
SAM.gov uses Login.gov for sign-in. Create the account with an email you control and set up its multi-factor authentication — you will use this login for every SAM.gov visit from now on.

2. Get your UEI through SAM.gov.
Every registered entity gets a Unique Entity Identifier (UEI). You request it inside SAM.gov as part of the registration process — you do not need a DUNS number anymore, and you do not get the UEI from anywhere else.

3. Start the entity registration.
Sign in at sam.gov and choose to register your entity for federal awards. Enter the legal name and physical address exactly as they appear on your records.

4. Pass entity validation.
SAM.gov checks your name and address against its validation sources. If what you enter does not match, you may be asked for supporting documents showing the same name and address.

5. Enter your taxpayer information.
Your EIN/TIN and the name tied to it are checked against IRS records. Type both exactly as the IRS has them — punctuation, abbreviations and all.

6. Add your business details.
Enter your NAICS codes, your business start date, and your banking information for electronic payments. A CAGE code is assigned to U.S. entities as part of this process.

7. Complete the representations and certifications.
This is a series of statements about your business — size, ownership, and compliance answers that federal buyers rely on. Read each one and answer truthfully; these are legal certifications.

8. Submit and watch your status.
After submission, your registration moves through validation. Check its status in SAM.gov rather than starting over — duplicates create more delay, not less.

How long it takes — honestly

Entering your information takes under an hour when your documents are gathered. The wait is validation: after you submit, processing often takes about 1–2 weeks, and sometimes longer. That is a general expectation, not a promise — a clean registration moves faster, and an IRS name mismatch can add weeks while you correct records. Plan your first bids around an active registration, not a submission date.

Mistakes that stall first-timers

  • Using a DBA or shortened name instead of the exact legal name on IRS records.
  • Typos in the EIN or entering the owner's Social Security number where the business EIN belongs (sole proprietors follow the on-screen instructions for their situation).
  • Choosing NAICS codes you cannot support — codes should describe work you actually do; buyers notice.
  • Letting the registration lapse. SAM.gov registrations must be renewed every year. An inactive registration can make a winning bid unusable.
  • Paying a random website for "registration." The registration is free at sam.gov. Pay only — if at all — for clearly described setup help.

Free help also exists: your local APEX Accelerator counsels businesses on government registrations at no charge, and the U.S. Small Business Administration offers guidance for federal sellers.

Questions

Is SAM.gov registration really free?

Yes. Registering your entity on SAM.gov costs nothing at sam.gov. Any paid service — including GovWin's $399 setup help — is optional assistance with the paperwork, never a fee for the registration itself.

How long does SAM.gov registration take?

After you submit, validation often takes about 1–2 weeks, and sometimes longer. The most common cause of delay is a legal name or taxpayer ID that does not match IRS records exactly.

Do I need a UEI before I register on SAM.gov?

No. The Unique Entity Identifier (UEI) is requested and assigned through SAM.gov itself as part of getting registered. You no longer need a DUNS number.

How often do I have to renew my SAM.gov registration?

Every year. A SAM.gov registration must be renewed annually to stay active, and it must be active when you bid and while you are being paid on a federal contract.
