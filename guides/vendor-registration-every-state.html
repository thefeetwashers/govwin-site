How to Register as a Government Vendor in Every State (and Your County and City)

No single vendor registration covers every government buyer in America — but there is a single pattern. Register at the levels where you want to sell, and five shared steps carry you through almost every portal in the country.

Four levels of government buying

Each level keeps its own vendor list, portal, and rules — one registration never covers another level. Start by deciding whose work you want.

1. Federal — SAM.gov

Selling to federal agencies — or subcontracting on federal prime contracts — starts with SAM.gov. Registration is free, always. Our SAM.gov registration guide walks through it. You need it for federal work, direct or through a prime.

2. State — your state vendor portal

Every state runs a vendor portal for its own agencies. You need it to sell to state government. It covers neither that state's counties and cities nor any other state — one registration per state you serve.

3. County — county purchasing

Counties buy constantly — roads, building services, vehicles, IT, supplies — and keep their own vendor registrations. You need it for county contracts. For many local businesses, it is the closest government revenue there is.

4. City — municipal procurement

Cities register vendors separately, through a purchasing department or a shared bid platform — and so do school districts and transit authorities. Register only with the local bodies you can actually serve.

Most vendors register at two or three levels — SAM.gov plus their home state, then nearby counties and cities.

The five steps every portal shares

1. Gather your business documents.
Every portal starts from the same file: legal business name and structure, tax ID (EIN), a current W-9, business and remittance addresses, a monitored contact email, and banking details for payment. Get it right once, and every later registration is faster.

2. Create your portal account.
Register on the buyer's official portal. Type names and tax details to match your records exactly — governments verify against their files, and mismatches are the most common reason registrations stall.

3. Choose your commodity and NAICS codes.
Portals describe what you sell in codes — commodity codes at state and local level, NAICS codes federally. Buyers search and send notices by these codes. Choose every code that honestly fits what you sell; skip codes you cannot deliver. This step decides which opportunities reach you.

4. Record your certifications.
If your business is small, minority-owned, woman-owned, veteran-owned, or in a HUBZone, certifications can connect you to set-aside work. Federal and state programs are separate — a state certificate is not a federal one — so record each one in the system that issued it.

5. Watch for bids, and keep the record alive.
Turn on notifications for your codes, check postings on a rhythm, and calendar your renewals — SAM.gov registrations expire, and local records go stale the same way. Treat every solicitation as our first-contract guide describes: read it fully, mind the questions deadline, submit exactly what is asked.

State portals: every state has one

The names differ; the job is the same. California vendors register through Cal eProcure. Texas runs Texas SmartBuy. Florida vendors use MyFloridaMarketPlace — covered step by step in our Florida guide. Pennsylvania has its PA Supplier Portal, Virginia runs eVA, Ohio buys through OhioBuys — and every other state and the District of Columbia runs an equivalent portal. The US territories — Puerto Rico, Guam, the US Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and the Northern Mariana Islands — run their own procurement offices as well.

To find yours safely, start from your state's official .gov website and navigate to its purchasing or procurement section, where vendor registration lives. That habit matters: look-alike commercial sites charge fees to "register you with the government," but official registration is done by you, on the government's own site. If a page is not on an official government domain, close it and start again.

Counties and cities: local is separate — and often closest

Below the state level, registration fragments — by design. Many counties, cities, and school districts run vendor registration and bid posting through regional platforms local governments share: names you will meet repeatedly include DemandStar, BidNet, OpenGov, and PlanetBids. Others register vendors from their own purchasing office's page. Either way, the route in is the same: the official county or city site, under Purchasing (sometimes Procurement or Business Services), holds the vendor registration instructions. One account on a shared platform can carry you to several of its buyers — but each buyer keeps its own vendor list, so confirm you are registered with the specific county or city, not just the platform.

Start with your home county and city, then neighboring jurisdictions your crews and routes can reach. A ready one-page capability statement makes the introductions that follow considerably easier.

Questions

Is SAM.gov registration really free?

Yes. SAM.gov registration — and renewal — is free at sam.gov. Firms that charge for SAM.gov help sell assistance, not the registration itself — the government never requires a third-party fee.

Does registering as a vendor in one state cover me in other states?

No. Every state runs its own vendor portal. Register in each state whose agencies you want to sell to; accounts, codes, and records never transfer between states.

Do I need county and city registration if I am registered with my state?

If you want local work, yes. Counties, cities, school districts, and other local buyers keep vendor registrations separate from the state portal — many through regional bid platforms or their own purchasing offices. State registration does not put you on a county or city vendor list.

Does vendor registration guarantee I will win government contracts?

No. Registration makes you eligible to be found, notified, and paid; each solicitation is still a competition on your price, qualifications, and how completely you follow the instructions. Registration is the entry ticket, not the award.
